Additive forcing of a random-frequency oscillator:
a catalogue of simulations, measurements and figures
Abstract
This document is an inventory. It lists every simulation run on the additively forced random-frequency
oscillator, the parameter grid each one covered, the quantities measured, and every figure produced,
together with an explicit guide to what is plotted on each axis and what each line, colour and marker
represents. Numbers quoted in the text are the measured values. No interpretation, verdict or conclusion is
offered anywhere in this document; the figures and the numbers are presented as they came out. Parametric
forcing is excluded except where a parametric run was used purely to calibrate a detector, which is stated
explicitly at that point.

1.1 Equations
Everything below is run on one of two systems.

The reduced two-dimensional system. In polar-like coordinates (h, ϕ), where h is an energy-like amplitude
variable and ϕ a phase, the additively forced oscillator is integrated as
¨X + ε ˙X + X = δ sin(ωt) +(random-frequency term), (1)
with the random-frequency term reduced, in the white-noise limit, to a state-dependent diffusion of strength
governed by σ and by the exponent parameter r. The stationary density of h in the unforced case is known in
closed form,
ps(h) = C (σ 2 + rh)−2/r, C = (2 − r)(σ 2)2/r−1, (2)
normalisable for r < 2 and of finite mean for r < 1.
The full three-dimensional system. Before the reduction, the frequency itself is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck
process At with correlation time τ, and the stiffness in (1) is A2
t . The reduced system is the τ → 0 limit. This
system is used in §4.
Parameters.
ε damping
σ noise amplitude of the random-frequency term
r multiplicative-noise exponent; r = 0 makes the system exactly linear
δ forcing amplitude
ω forcing frequency
τ correlation time of At (three-dimensional system only)
The natural frequency is ω0 = 1 throughout, so the free period is 2π ≈ 6.283 and the drive period is
Tdrive = 2π/ω.
1.2 Integrator
A symplectic (semi-implicit) Euler–Maruyama scheme is used, implemented in C and called through ctypes ;
the random number generator is xoshiro256++ . Forward Euler–Maruyama was rejected because it inflates
the energy by a factor (1 + ∆t2) per step; the Milstein correction vanishes for this system, so the scheme is of
strong order 1. When h exceeds 1012 the state is rescaled by 10−6 and the discarded factor is accumulated
in logh, so growth rates remain measurable without overflow. Common random numbers are used across a
sweep: at fixed seed, every (δ , ω) cell sees the same noise realisation, so differences between cells are not
seed noise.
Standard settings unless a section says otherwise: ∆t = 0.0025, output thinned by 80 (sample interval
0.2), and a burn-in that is discarded before any statistic is computed.
1.3 The observables
Spectral peak of X. The largest local maximum of the periodogram of X. A “sub-drive” variant excludes
frequency bands within ±3% of the first four drive harmonics mω, m = 1, . . . ,4, so that the free
oscillation can be tracked even when the drive line dominates.
Tracking slope. d(response frequency)/dω, fitted by least squares over a fixed window ofω ([1.8,2.2] for
the 2:1 window, [2.8,3.2] for the 3:1 window). A response that follows the drive at half its frequency
gives slope 1
2; one that ignores the drive gives slope 0.
Phase coherence. Rn =
⟨expi(ϕ − ωt/n)⟩
 for n = 1,2,3, averaged over the run. Because the reference
term depends on ω, the chance level does too, and nulls are matched on |ω/n − 1| rather than pooled.
Cycle period, phase-winding definition. The times at which the unwrapped phase ϕ advances by 2π; suc-
cessive differences are the cycle periods.

Cycle period, zero-crossing definitions. The times at which X crosses zero upward, found by linear inter-
polation between samples. Two counting rules are used and are compared in §7: raw, which accepts
every upward crossing, and hysteresis (a Schmitt trigger), which accepts an upward crossing only if X
has been below −hth since the previous accepted one, with hth = 0.25rms (X).
N, cycle length in drive periods. N = Tcycle/Tdrive = Tcycle ω/2π. In the unforced system Tcycle = 2π exactly,
so N = ω.
Growth rate. The least-squares slope of logh(t) against t, halved.
Sarle bimodality coefficient. b = (g2
1 +1)/g2 from the skewnessg1 and kurtosis g2 of the period distribution;
b = 5/9 for a uniform distribution.
Band powers of h(t). Fraction of the power of h(t) falling in narrow bands centred on ω/3, ω/2, ω and 2ω.

5 Calibration of the detector
Simulation S4. The identical measurement pipeline — same integrator, same run length T = 6000 and
burn-in 2000, same sub-drive peak finder, same fixed-window regression onω ∈ [1.8,2.2], same coherence
estimator — is applied to two forcings at ε = 0.05, σ = 1, r = 0.5, ω from 0.5 to 4.0 in steps of 0.05: additive
at δ ∈ {0,4,30} and, purely as a calibration reference, parametric at δ ∈ {0,1,4}. This is the only place in
this document where a parametric run appears, and it appears only to establish what the detector does when
the response does follow the drive.

7.1 The two counting rules
An upward zero crossing of X is located by linear interpolation between the two samples that bracket the sign
change. The raw rule counts every one. The hysteresis rule adds a Schmitt trigger: after an accepted crossing,
the signal must fall below −hth before another upward crossing can be accepted, with hth = 0.25rms (X)
computed from the run itself. Small wiggles that carry X back and forth across zero without a full excursion
are therefore counted by the raw rule and discarded by the hysteresis rule.
